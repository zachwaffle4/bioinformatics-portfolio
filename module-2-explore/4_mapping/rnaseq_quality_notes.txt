General impressions of the FastQC quality reports
RNA-seq reads from GEO GSE52778 (human airway smooth muscle cells, untreated and treated), 8 paired-end runs.
FastQC 0.12.1 was run on three of the 16 read files: SRR1039509_1, SRR1039509_2 and SRR1039516_1.
Reports are in qc/.

Overall: the reads are good quality and I would not trim them.

What the three files have in common
- 63 bp reads, no variation in length, ~49 % GC, Sanger/Illumina 1.9 quality encoding
- 21.2 million reads (SRR1039509) and 27.3 million reads (SRR1039516)
- 0 reads flagged as poor quality in any file
- Per base sequence quality, per sequence quality, per base sequence content, per sequence GC content, and adapter
  content all PASS in every file

Warnings and failures, and whether to worry

1. Sequence duplication levels: WARN in SRR1039509_1 and _2, FAIL in SRR1039516_1 (only 44.8 % of reads are
   distinct there; 50.4 % and 53.6 % in the two SRR1039509 files).
   Not a concern. In RNA-seq, a small number of highly expressed genes produce most of the reads, so many reads are
   identical by design. FastQC's duplication check assumes a genome-sequencing library. Reads should NOT be
   de-duplicated here, because that would erase real expression differences.

2. Per tile sequence quality: WARN in SRR1039509_1, FAIL in SRR1039509_2 (not flagged in SRR1039516_1).
   Some flowcell tiles had worse quality than the rest of the lane (the first reads in SRR1039508 show runs of N at
   '#' quality). Mate 2 is worse than mate 1, which is typical because read 2 is sequenced later in the run. Overall
   quality per base and per sequence still passes, so only a small share of reads is affected. I would note it
   and move on; aligners handle a few low-quality bases as mismatches.

3. Per base N content: WARN in SRR1039509_2 only. Same cause as above: no-calls at a few positions in a minority of
   reads. Not enough to need action.

4. Overrepresented sequences: WARN in SRR1039509_1 and _2, PASS in SRR1039516_1.
   - SRR1039509_1: one TruSeq adapter sequence, 0.86 % of reads.
   - SRR1039509_2: an Illumina single-end PCR primer sequence, 0.29 % (plus a 1-base variant at 0.11 %).
   These are leftover library-prep sequences, but in well under 1 % of reads. FastQC's adapter content module
   passes in every file because it only flags an adapter that reaches 5 % of reads at some position.

Do the reads need trimming?
No. Adapter content is far below the level where it matters, per base quality is high all the way along the 63 bp
reads, and the next step is STAR. STAR aligns in local mode and soft-clips bases at the ends of reads that do not
match the genome, so a few adapter or low-quality bases do not hurt mapping. Trimming 100 GB of FASTQs would take
hours and is unlikely to change the alignment rates noticeably. If the alignment rates come out low, trimming with
cutadapt (TruSeq adapter) would be the first thing to try.

Confirmed by the alignment
The untrimmed reads were aligned with STAR (results/star_mapping_summary.tsv). In all 8 samples, 92.4 to 94.7 % of
read pairs mapped uniquely (mean 93.9 %), so the leftover adapter, the N bases and the poor tiles did not hurt
mapping and trimming was not needed.

Limits of this check
Only 3 of 16 files were examined. The tutorial says the samples were run together and should look alike, and the
three reports are consistent with that, but the other files were not looked at.
