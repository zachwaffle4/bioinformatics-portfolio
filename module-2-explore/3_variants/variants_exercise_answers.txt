In-class exercises: variants tutorial (sea cucumber, 6 individuals, 1,000 reads each)
Commands and output are in variants_session.txt.

1. Map the untrimmed files to the genome. How do the alignments compare?

   Overall alignment rate per file (SRR6805880 ... SRR6805885):
     untrimmed:                22.0, 21.3, 22.3, 21.3, 21.2, 23.1 %   (mean 21.9 %)
     trimmed (TGCAG removed):  21.8, 21.8, 22.8, 20.8, 21.1, 23.4 %   (mean 21.95 %)

   The alignments are essentially the same. Trimming helps in three files and hurts in three, by at most 0.5
   percentage points, which is within what you would expect from noise with only 1,000 reads per file. Every read
   starts with TGCAG, which is probably the restriction-enzyme cut site (PstI: CTGCAG) from the library prep. If so,
   those 5 bases are real genome sequence and not an adapter, which would explain why removing them changes
   almost nothing. (This is my interpretation; I did not check it against the genome.) The low overall rate
   (~21 %) is expected because the genome and reads were subsampled separately.

2. Trim to 70 bp, map, and compare to the first trimmed reads.

   Command: cutadapt -g TGCAG -l 70  (removes the 5-prime TGCAG, then keeps the first 70 bases of what is left;
   80 bp reads -> 75 bp after adapter removal -> 70 bp)

   Overall alignment rate per file:
     trimmed to 70 bp:         21.1, 21.2, 22.6, 20.5, 20.5, 22.6 %   (mean 21.4 %)
     adapter-trimmed only:     21.8, 21.8, 22.8, 20.8, 21.1, 23.4 %   (mean 21.95 %)

   Trimming to 70 bp gives a slightly lower alignment rate in all six files (0.1 to 0.7 points lower). Shorter reads
   carry less sequence to anchor the alignment, so a few reads that aligned at 75 bp no longer do.

3. Change the angsd SNP p-value. How many more/less SNPs do we recover?

     SNP p-value    SNPs
     1e-1           54       (raised: more SNPs)
     1e-2            9       (the tutorial's value)
     1e-4            0       (lowered: fewer SNPs)
     1e-6            0

   Raising the p-value (less strict) recovers more SNPs; lowering it (more strict) recovers fewer, and none pass at
   1e-4 or below. With only 6 individuals and 1,000 reads each, mapped at ~21 %, there is very little coverage per
   site, so there is little statistical power to call a variant at a strict threshold. Two other p-values tried
   for the submission: 1e-1 (54 SNPs) and 1e-4 (0 SNPs); 1e-6 also gave 0.
